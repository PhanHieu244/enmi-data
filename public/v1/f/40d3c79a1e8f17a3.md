# The Vale Dossiers — author continuity and branching ledger

**Author document: contains all Part I spoilers. Do not give this file to a blind reviewer before their first playthrough.**

## Delivered story scope

`ch2.js`–`ch6.js` complete **Part I: The Portrait and the Crown / Bức chân dung và vương miện**, following the existing Chapter 1. They are native Enmi chapter data, not a separate player or a replacement mobile interface. Chapter 6 has four complete case endings. Each preceding chapter has a checkpoint, chapter ending and a real next-node link.

The English master text and Vietnamese translation were written together. English action labels are marked `silent:true`: the engine must not make Vale speak an instruction such as “Inspect the parcel” as dialogue. The actual spoken response follows as a native `say` line. Reading, listening, vocabulary, translation, history, stage and saved-state functions remain the engine’s native facilities. The optional language activities do not alter case outcomes.

**Parts II and III below are development scripts/designs, not executable or released chapters.** They must not appear as unlocked playable content in this build. They are independent cases; any shared-history reference follows the True Ending of Part I, without requiring the player to have chosen that ending in their own save.

## Chapter-by-chapter interaction and effect

### Chapter 2 — A House with Two Doors

The mobile location has front steps, cab stand and service gate. Inspect either entrance to establish the layout. Identify Hart through **either Bell or Finn**, with Reed checking the directory. Neither required clue depends on optional Chapter 1 questioning.

Bell is practical, protective of the house and impatient with gossip. Finn distinguishes usual behaviour from a timetable. Celia’s initial address changes if Vale’s name was actually supplied. Naming Matthias opens a frank conversation and an evening invitation; withholding the client keeps that part of the exchange unresolved. At Chapter 3 Hart will ask for confirmation rather than magically know the client.

Shared `c2_address` records only what either entrance route can establish. The replaced latch is described on its own hotspot and must not appear in the shared clue as though every player inspected it.

### Chapter 3 — An Unexpected Witness

Hart needs two witnesses, which explains the request for an extra person without predetermining the player’s role. Vale may sign under his true name or remain outside the formal record while Reed and the porter sign. The second option promises **no signature**, not anonymity: Reed audibly uses Vale’s full name. Both routes therefore fairly receive `c3_name_trace`.

The wedding is an observed fact. Hart’s prior knowledge is his own account. Celia’s warning is her direct account. None of them proves future harmlessness. The board asks the player to revise an understanding of motive and urgency, not award universal innocence.

The cuff pin is introduced through an ordinary repair while Vale helps with a window. It is a visible, specific recognition cue, reinforced by voice and by the later removal of a wig. It is not treated as a magical fingerprint.

Optional language activity: complete “Would you mind acting as a witness?” This is a polite request from the scene, with a Vietnamese explanation. A wrong answer changes no story branch.

### Chapter 4 — A Small Alarm

**Open route:** a direct conversation; terms drafted accurately; the photograph and recess shown voluntarily; permitted inspection of table/album, no drawers. Celia and Hart propose promises but cannot make Matthias or Pike agree on their behalf.

**Alarm route:** clerical cloak, grey wig and spectacles, lowered voice; brief staged street quarrel; apparent stumble; false alarm with stage smoke but no fire. Mrs Bell drops a bowl, and Vale pays for it. The scene does not claim that a simulated emergency has no consequences. The family’s invitation to speak is not converted into permission to search.

Both routes see the joint photograph and recess. The alarm route then offers a separate, explicit choice to take the photograph without permission. This is an inventory/state change, not merely a relationship-point variation. It opens the recovery ending or a later restitution scene.

Album and packed cases are optional. The album establishes that Celia has played male roles. It supports a hypothesis in Chapter 5 but is never required for the canonical ending. The theatrical ship is a **painted prop**, not an actor standing on a flat backdrop.

### Chapter 5 — Someone at Your Door

The passer-by’s line and two-finger salute are presented exactly when Vale observes them. Following yields only a departure by cab; no destination is invented. Calling out yields an evasive reply. Recording the greeting preserves it without asserting identity.

`present` lets the player correct a draft assertion contradicted by the greeting. The case board distinguishes public availability of Vale’s name from proof of a private leak. With the optional theatre clue it also permits a hypothesis about Celia, explicitly short of confirmation.

If holding the photograph, the player chooses between keeping it **for delivery** and physically returning it through Bell. Returning drops the photograph from inventory, records Bell’s receipt and permits a supported-facts ending; neither Celia nor Bell has to forgive the deception.

Without the photograph, there are three plans: verify before reporting; complete the witnessed agreement if an offer exists; or send an unsupported accusation. The agreement option needs a real current-run draft. Every run has a way forward without optional evidence.

Optional listening activity revisits the conditional warning. It tests the condition, not whether the player agrees with Celia or the king, and changes no ending.

### Chapter 6 — What a Portrait Is Worth

- **True Ending `c6_true`:** photograph not retained for delivery; read Bell-witnessed letter and inspect the separate portrait; connect the letter to the heard greeting; report supported facts and retract any earlier unsupported claim. Celia and Hart have left. The king agrees to send no more men. Vale keeps the offered portrait. Earlier mistakes can be corrected; completion of optional language tasks, every hotspot, a global truth collection or flawless behaviour is not required.
- **Agreement `c6_settlement`:** open discussion produced terms; Chapter 5 arranged and confirmed the meeting; Matthias agrees; Pike consents after reading; the two copies and photograph are checked; both signatures occur before transfer; a numbered sealed packet goes to Pike. This is a good alternative resolution, not a lower moral score. “True” denotes the adaptation’s chosen continuing history.
- **Recovery `c6_client`:** Vale chose to retain the taken print for delivery. Matthias receives it. Vale can disclose the method or accept praise, and always distinguishes the recovered print from proof no copies exist. Celia’s alternate letter says the photo is missing and does **not** claim she still holds it. Reed records the cost of the method.
- **Open file `c6_unresolved`:** after the final evidence, Vale still asserts more than it establishes and seeks further intrusion. Bell ends the invitation. No verified address or magically obtained photograph appears. This is an ending, not an engine dead end; replay is available.

The final deduction has a continuation after a failed attempt; it does not trap a player who has already read the relevant evidence. It never borrows knowledge from another playthrough.

## State and replay contract

Current-run inventory and flags govern all gates. Persistent `truth`/ending collections never grant facts to a run that did not observe them. Item IDs have chapter prefixes to avoid collisions. Shared frames, flags and older Chapter 1 saves remain the engine owner’s migration responsibility.

Important state: `c1_route`, `c1_contract`, `c2_approach`, `c2_name_given`, `c2_celia_trust`, `c3_role`, `c3_celia_trust`, `c4_route`, `c4_settlement_offer`, `c4_photo_taken`, `c4_boundary_broken`, `c5_returned`, `c5_plan`, `c5_report_corrected`, and final `p1_ending` / `p1_true`.

The evidence board does not mutate the physical world: possession changes only at the taking, return or delivery scene. “Record a clue” and “take an object” remain distinct in narration. Settlement hotspot order cannot transfer the frame before both checks: signing and sealing occur in the shared step after exploration finishes.

## Parts II–III: development design, not playable chapters

These plans extend the earlier series outline. Names below are new adaptation names. No item, accusation or ending from Part I supplies evidence in either new case. A later desk scene may show Celia’s separate portrait as continuity from `c6_true`; the case must remain understandable to a new player.

### Part II — The Room Beyond the Wall / Căn phòng bên kia tường

**Six planned chapters.** Client Lydia Marsh reports the earlier death of her sister Alice and a new order to sleep in Alice’s former room. Her guardian Dr Edmund Rusk has control of the house and an apparent financial interest. Groundskeeper Owen Hale knows repairs and access; housekeeper Ruth Dyer knows household routines. None is introduced with a guilt score. Rusk may become an early suspect; the remaining question must concern a testable method and how to protect Lydia, not pretend his menacing manner is a late surprise.

| Chapter | Playable intention | Consequence to carry |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — A Knock Before Dawn | Listen to Lydia, arrange a timeline of what she heard, saw and learned later; ask permission before inspecting personal papers | Her exact last-word memory remains testimony, not an authenticated code |
| 2 — The House at Blackthorn | Move among yard, corridor and bedrooms; compare entry routes and ask staff separately | Choose which access route Vale can verify before nightfall; no single optional hotspot contains the whole solution |
| 3 — The Room She Must Use | Read the room-change explanation and financial papers; test who can corroborate each claim | Motive and method remain separate board questions; exposing an unsupported accusation may close one interview but leave another source |
| 4 — The Silent Bell | Inspect objects with stated functions; trace where a cord and opening actually lead | Record a physical mismatch before building the night plan; an attractive object is not automatically a clue |
| 5 — A Night Without Light | Place Lydia somewhere safe, position observers and choose what to watch using already gathered evidence | Safety does not depend on fast reading or a one-second tap; wrong placement produces an observable warning and a chance to withdraw |
| 6 — Beyond the Vent | Compare the night observation with the room plan, then choose what can be reported and who can corroborate it | Distinguish rescue, a supported accusation and an incomplete explanation |

**Planned endings:** a supported-method resolution with Lydia safe (canonical); a safe withdrawal with the method unresolved; a premature confrontation that protects her immediately but loses access to proof; an incomplete accusation that the final report explicitly limits. A bad inference must not make the narration secretly change the room layout to punish the player.

**Fair clue design:** provide two independent ways to establish access and the room order. Record what a witness failed to hear only after establishing their position and normal expectations. A red herring should be a true fact with a limited implication, not a forged author statement. The last words can suggest a hypothesis; the physical observation must decide it.

**Research and release gate:** verify the proposed animal behaviour, sensing, movement and handling before choosing a final implementation of the inherited mechanism. Do not present every detail of the original tale as established biology. If an element cannot support a fair physical inference, revise the fictional mechanism openly in the design instead of hiding the inconsistency in dialogue. No operational mechanism or lethal sequence has been authored for this build.

### Part III — The Horse That Vanished / Con ngựa biến mất

**Five planned chapters.** The missing racehorse is Ember Star; the dead trainer is Thomas Greaves. Owner Lady Elin Wycliffe wants the horse recovered before the race. Stable worker Ben Larkin, rival trainer Felix Dorn and veterinary assistant Nora Finch provide different parts of the evidence. The horse’s disappearance and the trainer’s death must first be treated as two questions; one accusation should not automatically solve both.

| Chapter | Playable intention | Consequence to carry |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — An Empty Stall | Walk the stable, record the last confirmed sightings and ask each worker what they personally checked | A timeline shows gaps rather than inventing exact times; decide which witness to revisit first |
| 2 — The Moor at Daybreak | Follow available tracks on a small scene map, compare direction and condition, then inspect the discovery site | A track can be consistent with a route without identifying a person; weather and later traffic remain visible limitations |
| 3 — What the Dog Did Not Do | Establish the dog’s ordinary reactions and who was near enough to hear; compare accounts | Silence becomes useful only with a baseline; a familiar person remains a category, not a named culprit |
| 4 — The Trainer’s Other Life | Read financial records, ask about carried objects and compare animal-care reports with the timeline | A private debt supplies a possible motive, not proof of murder; distinguish a troubling plan from the actual cause of death |
| 5 — Before the Starting Bell | Verify the horse’s identity, connect both explanations and decide how to disclose them | Recovery and explanation receive separate statuses, so one success cannot silently certify the other |

**Planned endings:** recover the horse and support the explanation of the death (canonical); recover the horse but leave the death unresolved; explain the death while missing the recovery deadline; accuse a convenient suspect, then confront the contradiction in the record. The racing deadline is communicated in story choices; it must not penalise reading English slowly or using translation.

**Replay value:** investigation order changes who is available and which statement is heard before another. Evidence retains its original wording across routes. A later testimony may qualify an earlier account, but the game cannot retroactively rewrite the earlier line. Optional vocabulary, listening and speaking practice never changes the horse’s location or the cause of death.

**Writing gate:** these are chapter and consequence plans, not finished bilingual prose or executable nodes. They require a separate authoring, language-editing and independent UI-play cycle before being advertised as playable parts.
